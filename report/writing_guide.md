# How to Write the Report: Results, Discussion, Conclusion

A beginner's guide for our project. The **sentence examples are models to learn from**. Rewrite them in your own words, because the module penalises work that relies heavily on AI-generated text.

Word count: 1,500–2,000 words. The title, abstract, references and appendices are not counted. A sensible split:

| Section | Words | Uses sources? |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | ~400 | **Yes**: background, the gap, the RQs |
| Method | ~250 | Rarely |
| Results | ~400 | **No**: only your own data |
| Discussion | ~600 | **Yes**: compare your results with the literature |
| Conclusion | ~200 | Rarely or never |

**Key rule:** the **Results** section says *what* you found. The **Discussion** explains *why* and *what it means*, compared with other studies. Don't mix them.

## 1. What we can cite from each article

Each source below has one main fact you can safely use, plus its in-text citation. Paraphrase the fact. Don't copy it.

| Source | The fact you can use | In-text citation |
|---|---|---|
| Tinto (1975) | Students are more likely to stay at university and succeed when they are integrated both **academically** (with teachers and study) and **socially** (with peers). | (Tinto, 1975) |
| Crawford et al. (2024) | In a survey of 387 university students, those who relied on people for support had better outcomes (belonging, performance) than those who relied on AI chatbots. AI may *substitute* for human help. | (Crawford et al., 2024) |
| Hou et al. (2025) | Interviews with 17 computing students showed that help-seeking increasingly goes through ChatGPT instead of peers. Some students felt more isolated. | (Hou et al., 2025) |
| Hall and Liu (2022) | A review found little direct evidence that new technology (social media) replaces face-to-face time. Face-to-face time was already falling before social media. | (Hall and Liu, 2022) |
| Henderson et al. (2026) | In a survey of 6,960 students, about half used AI for feedback because it was easy to access, fast and lower-risk. They still trusted teacher feedback far more (90.5% vs 60.1%). | (Henderson et al., 2026) |
| Chan and Hu (2023) | 399 Hong Kong students were mostly positive about generative AI and valued its personalised, on-demand help. They also worried about accuracy, ethics and their own development. | (Chan and Hu, 2023) |
| Brandtzaeg, Skjuve and Følstad (2022) | Interviews with 19 chatbot users showed people can form a kind of "friendship" with AI, valued for availability and personalisation. They see it as different from human friendship. | (Brandtzaeg, Skjuve and Følstad, 2022) |
| Wang et al. (2025) | In a randomised controlled trial with 100 Chinese university students, a therapy chatbot reduced loneliness and depression over 7 days compared with a control group. | (Wang et al., 2025) |

**Citation tips:**
- **Integral** citation, where the author is part of the sentence: *Crawford et al. (2024) found that…*
- **Non-integral** citation, in brackets at the end: *…AI may replace human support (Crawford et al., 2024).*
- Use both kinds; the marking descriptors reward variety.
- Vary your reporting verbs: *found, reported, argued, suggested, showed, observed, concluded*.

## 2. RESULTS: what to write

**Purpose:** describe your data objectively. No sources, no explanations, no opinions.

**Structure:** organise by research question, and refer to each figure.

1. **One opening sentence:** number of respondents (40), their subjects, and how often they use AI (Q2: 100% use AI, 45% every day).
2. **Main RQ (Figure 1, Q3, Q4):**
   - 63% agreed that AI has reduced their in-person study time with classmates.
   - Only 45% agreed that AI could reduce face-to-face conversation in the future (38% disagreed).
3. **Sub-RQ1 (Figure 1 and Figure 3, Q5–Q8, Q12):**
   - Q6 is the strongest result: 70% prefer AI to office hours, and only 10% disagree.
   - Q5: 60% say their group brainstorms with AI. Q7: 63% feel more confident messaging.
   - Q8 split: 40% agree vs 40% disagree.
   - Q12: "available anytime" was the top reason (43%); "less embarrassed" was lowest (13%).
4. **Sub-RQ2 (Figure 2 and Figure 4, Q9–Q11, Q13):**
   - 55% feel comfortable treating AI like a friend, but only 15% say it makes them less lonely (68% disagree).
   - Q11 split: 40% vs 43%.
   - Q13: "no effect" 35%; "rely on AI too much" 30%.

**Useful language:**
- *As shown in Figure 1, …*
- *The majority of respondents (70%) …*
- *Just over half …*
- *Opinions were divided on …*
- *Only a small minority (13%) …*
- *In contrast, …*

**Example (rewrite in your own words):**
> As shown in Figure 1, the strongest agreement concerned help-seeking from teachers: 70% of respondents preferred asking an AI chatbot to explain course material rather than attending office hours, while only 10% disagreed.

**Avoid in Results:**
- explanations ("because students are lazy")
- citations
- the word "prove"

## 3. DISCUSSION: what to write

**Purpose:** interpret the results, compare them with the literature, explain them, and admit limitations. This section carries the most marks for critical thinking.

**A good discussion paragraph has four steps:**
1. **Restate** the key finding briefly.
2. **Compare** it with the literature: does it agree or disagree?
3. **Explain** possible reasons, using hedging language.
4. Optionally, a **limitation or implication**.

### Four discussion points you can make

**Point 1: AI is replacing academic contact more than social contact (Main RQ).**
- *Findings:* Q4 (63%) and Q6 (70%) were strong, while Q3 and Q11 were split.
- *Agrees with:* Crawford et al. (2024), who describe AI as substituting for human academic support, and Hou et al. (2025), who found help-seeking moving from peers to ChatGPT.
- *Contrasts with:* Hall and Liu (2022), who found little evidence that technology displaces face-to-face *social* time. This may explain why our social items were split.
- *Theory link:* Tinto (1975). If academic integration falls, first-year success could suffer, even if social life seems unaffected.

**Point 2: students choose AI for convenience, not shyness (Sub-RQ1).**
- *Findings:* Q12 shows "available anytime" at 43% and "less embarrassed" at only 13%.
- *Agrees with:* Henderson et al. (2026) and Chan and Hu (2023). Students value access, speed and on-demand help.
- *Critical point:* Henderson et al. (2026) also found students trust teachers far more than AI (90.5% vs 60.1%). Preferring AI for convenience does not mean students think it is better, so the two may complement each other rather than one replacing the other.

**Point 3: AI as a "friend", but not a cure for loneliness (Sub-RQ2).**
- *Findings:* Q9 shows 55% comfortable treating AI like a friend; Q10 shows 68% say it does not reduce loneliness.
- *Agrees with:* Brandtzaeg, Skjuve and Følstad (2022). AI friendship feels available, but users see it as different from human friendship. Crawford et al. (2024) found that support from AI did not give the same benefits as support from people.
- *Contrasts with:* Wang et al. (2025), whose chatbot reduced loneliness in Chinese students.
- *Possible reason:* their chatbot was a purpose-built therapy tool used for 7 days, while our students use general chatbots for study. **This is good critical thinking: explain the difference between studies.**

**Point 4: signs of dependence (link to the tutor's question).**
- *Finding:* 30% chose "rely on AI too much" (Q13).
- *Link:* Chan and Hu (2023) found students worried about effects on their personal development.
- Our data shows mainly a **preference** for AI in study tasks, with some signs of **dependence**.

### Limitations (one paragraph near the end of the Discussion)

- Small convenience sample (n = 40); Business students made up 45%, Arts/Humanities only 5%.
- The survey did not check year of study, so not all respondents may be first-year.
- Self-reported answers; the survey shows associations, not causes.
- Some items, such as Q8 and Q11, split evenly, so no firm conclusion is possible for them.

**Hedging language, which you must use:**
- *This may suggest… / It is possible that… / One explanation could be…*
- *These findings appear to be consistent with…*
- *This partly contrasts with…*

**Example (rewrite in your own words):**
> This finding is consistent with Crawford et al. (2024), who suggest that AI chatbots may substitute for human academic support. However, it partly contrasts with Hall and Liu (2022), who found little evidence that technology displaces face-to-face social time, which may explain why respondents were divided on Q11.

**Avoid in Discussion:**
- "AI causes loneliness"
- "this proves"
- introducing new results that weren't in the Results section

## 4. CONCLUSION: what to write

**Purpose:** close the report. Answer the research questions briefly and say what it means. **No new data, and usually no citations.**

**Structure (about 200 words):**
1. **Restate the aim** in one sentence.
2. **Answer each RQ in one sentence each:**
   - **Main RQ:** partly yes. AI appears to reduce face-to-face *academic* contact more than *social* contact.
   - **Sub-RQ1:** students prefer AI over teachers and group discussion for study tasks, mainly for convenience.
   - **Sub-RQ2:** students may treat AI like a friend, but most do not feel less lonely, and some feel over-reliant.
3. **Implication or recommendation:** for example, universities could make teacher help more accessible (more flexible office hours, online Q&A), or encourage group work that requires discussion.
4. **Main limitation plus future research:** for example, a larger sample with all first-year students, or comparing heavy and light AI users over time.

**Example opening (rewrite):**
> This study aimed to investigate whether AI chatbot use reduces first-year students' face-to-face interaction. The findings suggest that…

## 5. Quick checklist

- [ ] Results: numbers and figures only. No citations, no "why".
- [ ] Every figure is mentioned in the text ("As shown in Figure 2…").
- [ ] Discussion: each paragraph has a finding, a source comparison and an explanation.
- [ ] At least one source that **contrasts** your results: Hall and Liu (2022) or Wang et al. (2025).
- [ ] Hedging language throughout; no "prove" or "cause".
- [ ] Conclusion answers all three RQs; no new data.
- [ ] Every in-text citation appears in the reference list, and vice versa.
- [ ] 4–8 sources in total (we have 8).
