## Results

**Participants**

A total of 40 students completed the questionnaire. Almost half of the respondents studied Business (45%), followed by Science/Engineering (30%) and Arts/Humanities (5%), while the remaining 20% reported other subjects, such as Finance, Data Science and Pharmacy. All respondents reported using AI chatbots. Nearly half (45%) used them every day, 18% on five to six days a week, 25% on three to four days and 13% on one to two days; no respondent chose "never".

**Academic interaction**

Figure 1 presents responses to the six statements about AI chatbot use and face-to-face study interaction. The strongest agreement concerned help from teachers: 70% of respondents preferred asking an AI chatbot to explain course material rather than going to their teacher's office hours, while only 10% disagreed and 20% were neutral. A clear majority (63%) also agreed that AI chatbots had reduced the time they spend studying in person with classmates, compared with 30% who disagreed. Similarly, 60% reported that their group uses AI chatbots to brainstorm instead of discussing ideas with each other, and 63% felt that using AI to write or check messages had made them more confident when contacting classmates and teachers. In contrast, opinions were divided on two statements. The same proportion of respondents agreed and disagreed (40% each) that talking face-to-face with classmates requires more effort than asking an AI chatbot, and 45% agreed that AI chatbots could reduce face-to-face conversations between students in the future, while 38% disagreed.

**Reasons for using AI chatbots**

As shown in Figure 2, the most common reason for asking an AI chatbot instead of a classmate or teacher was that it is available at any time (43%). This was followed by the belief that AI gives better answers (25%) and that it is faster (18%). Only a small minority (13%) chose feeling less embarrassed, and one respondent (3%) gave another reason.

**Social interaction and loneliness**

Figure 3 shows the responses to the three statements about social interaction and loneliness. Just over half of the respondents (55%) felt comfortable treating an AI chatbot like a friend, whereas 28% disagreed. However, only 15% agreed that talking to an AI chatbot makes them feel less lonely, while a large majority (68%) disagreed. Respondents were almost evenly divided on whether they would have talked to more people if AI did not exist, with 40% agreeing and 43% disagreeing.

**Effect on social life**

Finally, Figure 4 summarises what respondents saw as the biggest effect of AI chatbots on their social life. The most frequent answer was "no effect" (35%), closely followed by "it makes me rely on AI too much" (30%). A further 20% reported that AI helps them communicate in another language, whereas fewer respondents felt that it makes them spend less time with friends (10%) or helps them talk to new people (5%).
